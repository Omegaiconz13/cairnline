**Reading the permission column:** *Yes* means compatible with a closed paid
product provided the copyright/permission notice (and, for Vela, the visible
attribution) is kept. *Local/browser only* and *dev-only* are AGPL §13
boundaries, not permission to serve the code. *Unverified* means no licence file
was found, so no permission is inferred.

**Not a repository, but decisive:** the **LuxAlgo Library** indicator source is
**CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** (read 2026-11-18). NonCommercial + ShareAlike: using a study
on your own account is fine; selling it, redistributing it for a fee, or building
it into a paid product is not. The Library's *written* pages are a separate,
more permissive licence (free to quote with attribution); the **code** is the
CC BY-NC-SA part that matters here.

### LSE Data 3.0 — the unused-API map (what we call vs what is available)

Every call is `GET /v3/<call>` with `X-API-Key`. Browsers may not call it
(cross-origin is refused), so it belongs in `market_service.py`.

**Call sites actually present (checked 2026-11-18):** `grep -rhoE
"client\.[a-z_]+\(" server/*.py` finds exactly `candles`, `preview`, `usage`,
`economic_calendar`, `cot`, `options_chain`, `options_flow`. Everything marked
"no" below has no call site in `server/`. The installed client is `lse` **3.0**
and exposes every method named here; whether the key is *entitled* to a surface
is a plan question (L1/L2/L3 and some exports are paid), which is why the
"Used by us" column records calls, not a claim about the plan's allowance.

| Surface | Calls | Used by us | What it would unlock |
|---|---|---|---|

**Operational facts that shape any design:**
- One download at a time per key, on **every** plan (`connections: 1`).
- Free plan observed: 60 calls/min, ~12.5 GiB/week, 9 markets.
- `429` carries the reason (`calls-exhausted`, `allowance-exhausted`,
  `connections-exhausted`, `exports-busy`); the client honours `Retry-After` once.
- Exports over 100 MB become a job (24 h, resumable, SHA-256 verified).

platform: licensed CME data (16 y tick, L2/L3, ~1 TB) plus MT5/cTrader broker

1. **PineTS / Vela-pinets / pinets-cli — AGPL-3.0.** Running them locally or in
   the user's browser is the low-risk case. Serving them from *our* servers to
   paying users triggers AGPL §13: network users must be offered the
   Corresponding Source of the version they interact with. The paid product does
   not do that, so **PineTS is not served to paying users without a commercial
   licence** (or full AGPL compliance); the local rail stays local. Evidence:
   the full AGPL text is at `server/node_modules/pinets/LICENSE`, and the

   shipping it closed-source at any size".

   multi-user Cairnline serving LSE candles must negotiate first
   (support@londonstrategicedge.com).
4. **Vela — Apache-2.0 + NOTICE.** The attribution watermark must remain, or an
   equivalent visible "Vela™" link must appear on the same page.
   *Current state: compliant* — `app/dashboard/vendor/vela/NOTICE` and

   are fine to vendor and ship with their copyright and permission notice; names

7. **`brue` — MIT but 0.0.1.** Pin the version; do not build the product's

8. **`brue-connect` — no public repository, therefore no licence.**

## 7. Known limitations on the chart (as built)

**Pane management is supported — an earlier note here said it was not.** That
note claimed `chart.panes` exposed only read-only `paneOrder` / `maximizedPaneId`.
Reading the vendored bundle shows otherwise: `chart.panesControl` is a fluent
facade over the pane controller with `supported`, `list()`, `collapse(id, on)`,
`maximize(id | null)`, `setHeightWeight(id, weight)` and `moveIndicator(id, pane)`,
gated on the renderer capability `paneManagement` (true in this build). The
renderer also draws hover controls per pane — `.vela-pc-btn` with
`data-role="up" | "down" | "collapse" | "maximize"` and the titles "Move panel
up/down", "Collapse panel", "Maximize panel", "Restore panel" — which is how
Vela itself exposes the same operations. Cairnline now shows a maximise and a
collapse button on every own-pane study in the Indicators menu, wired to that
facade; Vela's own hover controls keep working unchanged.

**Extended 2026-11-18.** Every active study now has visibility, placement and
geometry controls, not just inputs:

- **Visibility.** An eye per study calls `handle.setVisible(bool)`. The study,
  its pane and its inputs stay; hiding only stops its session.
- **Placement.** `handle.moveTo('price')` and `handle.moveTo({ newPane: true })`
  move a study between the price pane and a pane of its own. A fresh pane starts
  at weight 1, so the saved height is restored in the same action.
- **Height.** `panesControl.setHeightWeight(paneId, weight)` through a −/+ stepper
  (clamped 1–8). Vela's own hover controls still work and are mirrored into the
  same store via `pane:changed` / `indicator:moved` / `indicator:visibility`.
- **Persistence.** `localStorage.cairnline_indicator_layout` keeps one record per
  study (`hidden`, `placement`, `weight`, `collapsed`, `maximized`) and is
  re-applied after a reload. The pass runs one frame after the studies are added,
  because a pane's renderer row is created asynchronously and a height set in the
  same tick is dropped.

Coverage after the exclusion list: **75 of 77** registry studies plus three EMA
presets. `volume` and `vpvr` are excluded in `terminal.js` (`EXCLUDED_STUDIES`):
they add, remove and expose inputs, but `chart.inspect()` shows no series, fill,
line or polyline for either, on EURUSD or on BTCUSD where the provider reports
volume. `zigzag` draws nothing on FX but does draw polylines on BTCUSD, so it
stays — that is a parameter/data-window effect, not a broken study.

What is still missing, honestly:

- **Pane heights have a stepper, not a drag handle.** Vela exposes
  `setHeightWeight()`, so this is UI polish, not an API gap.
- **The no-Vela fallback renderer still draws EMA overlays only.** Panes, and
  therefore these controls, only exist on the Vela path.
- Studies that need a data field the feed does not carry (per-bar volume-by-price)
  are excluded rather than approximated; `availableNativeIndicators()` is also
  consulted at mount so a provider-level `supported: false` is filtered too.

### 8.2 The options math it publishes

This is the most useful page they have, because it is the contract for every
number their suite shows — and it tells us exactly what our feed cannot support.

**GEX.** `GEX = Γ × OI × S² × 0.01` per contract, i.e. dollars of hedging flow per
**1% move**. Summed over the board it gives net GEX; its sign is the **dealer
regime** (long gamma above zero, short gamma below).

**Signing the exposure is an estimate, stated as one.** Two bases:

- *Dealer flow* (their default): reconstruct each contract's dealer inventory from
the public tape, one contribution per print — `−v` for a taker buy, `+v` for a
taker sell — downweight privately negotiated blocks, then clamp the fold to the
contract's own open interest. A per-instrument **coverage** gate decides the
basis: coverage ≥ 0.6 → dealer flow, below → the naive convention.
- *Open interest* (the industry convention and explicit fallback): dealers long
  calls, short puts, so calls sign positive and puts negative.

Never blended per contract: each instrument is wholly one basis or the other, and
every board discloses the split ("Dealer flow · N% naive fallback", weighted by
unsigned OI gamma notional). History is seeded up to **185 days** per coin.

**Landmarks.** Call wall = largest call OI above spot; put wall = largest put OI
below spot; **gamma flip** = the price nearest spot where net GEX crosses zero,
*re-derived by repricing every contract at that price* — and they explicitly say
the running-sum-of-strike-GEX zero crossing is **not** the flip (it is shown
separately as "Cumulative GEX"). Max pain stays OI-based and re-uses the
settlement definition, `argmin_K Σ OI × payout(K)`, whichever GEX basis is live.

**Greeks.** Venue-published where available, otherwise one Black-Scholes
evaluation: `d1 = (ln(S/K) + ½σ²T) / (σ√T)`, `d2 = d1 − σ√T`,
`Δ = N(d1)` (call) or `N(d1) − 1` (put), `Γ = φ(d1) / (S σ √T)`,
`Vega = S φ(d1) √T / 100`. Net GEX, the flip and the regime always use the
model, so they cannot disagree with each other.

**Volatility.** ATM IV is the mean of the call and put mark IV at the strike
nearest that expiry's **forward**; the smile is OTM-stitched (puts below the
forward, calls above); RR25 = call IV − put IV at 25 delta.

**Conventions that matter to us.** Coin units for OI and volume; Deribit-only
for the dealer-flow estimate; live panels poll every 60 s by default (30–600
configurable); the options heatmap reconstructs history from stored IV rather
than live greeks; and the whole suite names its limitations rather than
guaranteeing the estimate.

**What this means for Cairnline.** LSE's free options feed supplies prints with
size, premium, IV, delta and gamma, plus a chain with strikes, expiries and
greeks — but **no open interest**, and no exchange condition codes. So:

| Their number | Can we compute it honestly? |
|---|---|
| Tape-weighted gamma exposure by strike | **Yes** — shipped in `server/options_flow.py`, labelled `tape_contracts_traded` |
| Max pain | Only tape-weighted; ours says so in the payload |
| Gamma flip, call/put walls, IV smile | **Built 2026-11-18** from the LSE chain: the flip is repriced at each candidate price with Black-Scholes gamma from each leg's IV/dte; walls and smile use the same traded-contract weights |
| Term structure, options surface, vol matrix, implied move | Yes, computable from the same chain — not built yet |
| Dealer flow, dealer positioning, coverage split, OI-based GEX | **No** — needs open interest and a tape we can fold by aggressor. Do not approximate these, and do not use their names |

### 8.3 wrun — their developer engine, and why it competes with our Pine rail

or on their servers when the code never leaves OpenMarket — with alerts in their
cloud. Their comparison page is blunt: *Pine works from candles; a wrun
indicator gets the market under the candles.* That is the same argument we would
have to answer if a user asks why our rail is PineTS.

### 8.5 Guardrails for anything OpenMarket-inspired

1. **No licence, no code.** No repository exists and none of the pages read
   carries a licence instrument, so the default is all rights reserved. Do not
   paste their docs or their indicator packages into this product, and do not
   vendor a registry package — those belong to their authors.
2. **Standard maths is not their IP.** Black-Scholes greeks, `Γ × OI × S² ×
   0.01`, max pain as an `argmin` over payout, and 25-delta risk reversals are
   textbook. Reimplement them and cite the definition, not the page.
3. **Do not borrow their estimate's name.** "Dealer positioning", "dealer flow"
   and the coverage split describe an inference from Deribit's tape. We have no
   OI and no aggregate tape we can fold that way, so using those words would be
   a claim we cannot support. Our exposure stays `tape_contracts_traded`.
4. **Their data moat is not a code gap.** OPRA, CME chains, Deribit and
   prediction markets are feed contracts. Reading their docs does not move our
   coverage; buying a feed does.
5. **Everything in §4 still applies**, in particular the LSE no-redistribution
   term: a hosted multi-user Cairnline may not serve LSE candles without written
   permission, and an OpenMarket-style public data API is exactly that.
